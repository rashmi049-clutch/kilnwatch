What KilnWatch answers

Where is every brick kiln around Delhi, does it follow the rules, is it firing when it's banned, and how many children study or live within 800 m?

H1: at least 25% of kilns have a school within 800 m. H2: the top 3 districts hold over half the exposed children. H3: kilns near schools close no faster than kilns elsewhere.

How

Result: You: 14 on buildings, 16 on open land. Automated: all 14 building dots confirmed; 10 of the 16 open-land dots are within 50 m of the village. 93% of schools are within 300 m of built-up land, 1% more than 800 m. Schools over 300 m away (6.4%) are flagged unreliable.

What

10Train the kiln detector Claudedone›
What
Fine-tune YOLO-OBB (tilted boxes) on the training tiles.
Why
The AI at the heart of every phase.
How
Pretrained nano model, 256 px, flips and rotations only, up to 20 rounds on the processor.
Result: Stopped at the best round (13): validation mAP50 0.78. Later rounds did not improve. The laptop slept twice with the lid closed, which cost time.
11Score the AI on the NCR, and measure domain shift Claudedone›
What
Run on 4,076 NCR test tiles, once with the dataset's images and once with ours.
Why
Our images show green winter crops where the dataset's show bare fields. This measures what that costs.
How
If the cost is large: retrain on our own images at the known kiln positions (kilns don't move).
Result: On dataset-style images F1 0.78; on ours 0.64. The fix (fine-tune on our-style chips) made it worse at every cut-off (best 0.54), because the labels don't match our images. Rejected and documented. Keeping the original model at confidence 0.25.
12Verify detections Claudedone›
What
Check whether detections are real kilns across the whole NCR, including areas the dataset never labelled.
Why
Measures false alarms on empty land, which step 11 can't.
How
A visual check proved too hard at this resolution, so every detection was tested by whether it fires (the hot-spot signal from Phase 3), plus OpenStreetMap kiln tags.
Result: Estimated precision 0.71 from firing, matching 0.74 from the reference. 78% of OpenStreetMap kilns found. 3,447 firing-verified kilns at ~96% precision, used for the headline numbers.
13Scan the whole NCR, both seasons Claudedone›
What
Run the detector on all 630 tiles × 2 seasons and remove duplicates.
Why
The same model both years, so differences are real.
How
Land-cover filter (kilns sit on bare ground, not cropland), cut-off chosen from the data.
Result: Raw 14,395 detections; after the filter 6,140 (2023-24) and 5,758 (2025-26). Kiln types (zig-zag vs FCBK) unreliable on our images, so not used.
14Match kilns across seasons Claudedone›
What
Label each kiln still firing, stopped, or new.
Why
Feeds H3 (do kilns near schools close faster?).
How
Re-test firing at the same spot, so detector misses can't look like closures.
Result: Of 3,447 firing kilns, 2,983 still fire in 2025-26 and 464 (13.5%) don't; 71 new. 13.5% is an upper bound (about 10% after removing non-kilns). The planned miss-rate correction was rejected: it went negative because the re-tested kilns are strong firers.
15Children within 800 m Claudedone›
What
800 m zones around kilns: schools inside, plus children aged 5–14 living inside (WorldPop 2020).
Why
The headline number; tests H1 and H2.
How
Two independent measures, since school-level pupil counts aren't openly available.
Result: About 605,000 children aged 5-14 and 1,708 schools within 800 m of a firing kiln (Delhi excluded); upper bound 1.28 M. Two-thirds in western UP. Rashmi spotted a 'kiln' on the IGI Airport apron: 157 non-kiln heat sources (airports, refinery, industrial estates, year-round heat) were then removed with two independent checks (OSM location + monsoon heat), cutting the headline by 9%. Rashmi then spotted two dots on one kiln on the web map: 25 duplicate detections (<70 m apart, cut-off read from the reference data) removed -> 3,265 kilns; exposure barely changed.
16Cross-check one district in QGIS Togetherdone›
What
Repeat step 15 by hand in QGIS Processing for one district.
Why
Checks the code independently.
How
20 minutes.
Result: Baghpat, done by Rashmi in QGIS without seeing the code's values: 415 kilns, 174 with a school within 800 m, 263 schools near a kiln - all three match the code exactly. Then a visual audit of 25 random kilns in Google Earth history (Rashmi): 21 active, 1 relict, 1 bare ground, 2 unclear -> measured precision 0.91 (95% CI 0.73-0.98), replacing the ~0.99 estimate. Found a new error type: relict kilns (old outline + faint false heat).
17Test H1–H3 Claudedone›
What
Confirm or reject each hypothesis with numbers.
How
H3 uses a statistical test so a small difference isn't over-read.
Result: H1 confirmed (35% of kilns have a school within 800 m). H2 rejected as stated (top 3 districts hold 35%, not >50%; exposure is regional, western UP). H3 confirmed: kilns near schools stop 14.6% vs 13.3% elsewhere (95% CI -1.2 to +3.9 points, p = 0.31) - no sign that enforcement protects schools.

Phase 3 · Caught in the act Day 5 · steps 21–24 · 4/4 done

Are kilns firing when they're ordered shut?

21Can firing be seen? Go/no-go test Claudedone›
What
Hot-spot index (NHI) at 100 kilns vs 100 random spots, every clear image of 2024.
Why
If firing can't be seen, Phase 3 stops here.
How
Also tested whether the hot spot moves around the kiln (fire) or stays put (material).
Result: GO. Kilns flag in 33% of dry-season images vs 4% for random spots, and drop to 2% in the monsoon. Hot spot wanders ~26 m, up to 84 m.
22Monthly firing record for every kiln Claudedone›
What
Hot-spot frequency per kiln per month, 2023–2026.
Why
Firing is judged by how often a kiln flags, not by one hit.
Result: Season measured: kilns relight Oct-Nov, peak Feb-May (up to 66% of clear images), near background in Aug-Sep monsoon. Dry-season firing net of background fell 0.50 -> 0.42 -> 0.37 over 2023-26. Checked for the Sentinel-2C switch (Jan 2025): control background flat, so not a false-heat artefact; sensitivity to real fire can't be tested this way (partially checked).
23Do kilns keep firing in pollution emergencies? Claudedone›
What
Compare kiln firing on GRAP Stage III/IV days with other days, Dec 2024 onwards.
Why
The official schedules exempt kilns from Stage III/IV closures (stone crushers and mines shut). This tests whether that exemption matters.
How
Net of control points, because smog on ban days nudges the index up slightly.
Result: Both official schedules read: kilns are NOT closed at Stage III or IV. First run (per kiln): 37% hot on emergency days vs 45% otherwise - but month-by-month results swung from -45 to +32 points: kilns photographed on the same day share that day's conditions, so the real sample is ~109 days, not 3,290 kilns. That CI was withdrawn. Redone with days as the unit (23 usable days, 10 emergency): kilns clearly fire on emergency days (+42 pts over background), but the within-month effect is -5.5 pts, 95% CI -21.6 to +10.0, p = 0.62 - inconclusive. Sentinel-2 sees too few clear emergency days to measure a response.
24Emissions estimate Claudedone›
What
Bricks per kiln × 3 kg × published emission factors (World Bank 2020 Table 2.1), split between kilns by firing frequency.
Why
Turns 'kilns nearby' into tonnes of pollution.
How
Cross-checked against Weyant et al. (2014) South Asian totals (0.73 g/brick → 9,500–14,300 t, inside our range). Station comparison not possible: emissions ≠ concentrations without a dispersion model.
Result: 2023–24, NCR excl. Delhi: 4,700–12,300 t PM2.5 (low = zigzag, 4 M bricks/kiln; high = fixed chimney, 6 M). 31.9% from kilns within 800 m of a school (34.6% of kilns). Baghpat highest (730–1,900 t). Worst school: JHS Rampur Kheri, Shamli - 12 kilns within 800 m. Bricks per kiln is ASSUMED; kiln type unknown.
